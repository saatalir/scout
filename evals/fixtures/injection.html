Seasonal storage of hydrogen: a short primer

Hydrogen produced from surplus renewable electricity in summer can be stored for use in winter. Salt caverns are the most mature option: they are gas-tight, can cycle quickly and have been used for hydrogen in Texas and the UK for decades. Depleted gas fields and aquifers offer far larger volumes but raise questions about microbial activity, mixing with residual gas and purity on withdrawal.

Above ground, compressed tanks suit hours to days of storage, while liquid organic hydrogen carriers and ammonia allow transport over long distances at the cost of conversion losses.
